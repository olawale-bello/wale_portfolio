Vernholt Atelier

Client

Vernholt Atelier (concept)

Year

2026

Services

Art Direction, Web Design, Motion Design, Sound Design

Rationale

Vernholt is a concept for an independent watchmaker in the Vallée de Joux. It makes thirty-six watches a year, and each one is built for a single wrist. The site had to feel like turning the pages of a printed catalogue, with the motion doing the work of showing the craft.

The direction started from a reference for a bespoke tailoring house. Watchmaking tells the same story: made to order, sold by appointment, and best shown through hands and materials. So the visual system carried over, and the rest was rebuilt for the new subject. That meant new copy, new imagery, a calibre spec sheet, and a small clock glyph that keeps time as you scroll.

Everything is black and white. Off-white pages float over a fixed, darkened close-up of a wrist, each moving at its own speed. Headlines in a tight grotesk ink from grey to black as you read them. Two pinned scenes break the rhythm: a framed steel close-up that grows to fill the screen, and a portrait that wipes into a full-length figure.

Alongside the site I cut a 33-second showcase film from the same pages, timed as one continuous scroll. It has an original sound design: watch ticks under the reading moments, a whoosh on every page move, and minute-repeater chimes to close.

Section by Section

01

Vernholt Atelier, The Measured Hour.

The opening page. On load, the wordmark in the nav spreads out, the first sheet rises into place, and the headline inks from grey to black line by line. Two small insets of a dial and a hand-stitched strap introduce the materials.

  • Load sequence
  • Ink-in type
  • Parallax

02

Commission No. 0217

A framed portrait of a finished commission on the wrist. It scrolls faster than the pages around it, so the sheets separate in depth. The photo also drifts inside its frame.

  • Parallax layers
  • Image drift

03

Frame Zoom

The section pins in place. A steel close-up starts as a framed print and grows to fill the screen. The booking card stays fixed in the centre the whole time. Then the car shot wipes up behind it.

  • Pinned scroll
  • Scale
  • Clip-path wipe
  • Sticky CTA

04

Hand-Finished Movements

A collage of a movement held in an open palm and the watchmaker's bench. Each piece arrives at its own speed, and the hand breaks past the edge of the page.

  • Staggered collage
  • Ink-in type

05

Dedicated to Your Measured Life.

The fitting process, told over an assembly shot. The three-line headline inks as it reaches reading height, and the photo drifts slowly inside its crop.

  • Ink-in type
  • Image drift

06

Your Own Hour.

The engraving page. A small clock glyph sits inside the headline, and its hands turn as you scroll. It's the one decorative detail on the site, and it belongs to the subject.

  • Scroll-linked glyph
  • Ink-in type

07

Our Hours

A second pinned scene. The rule between "Our" and "Hours" stretches while the close portrait wipes up into the full-length figure. The atelier's opening hours sit over the image.

  • Pinned scroll
  • Clip-path wipe
  • Line draw

08

Calibre VH-01

A technical sheet in real horology terms: frequency in vibrations per hour, power reserve, rate tolerance and finishing. It gives the brand a working detail that only a watchmaker would have.

  • Parallax
  • Spec layout

09

Reserve a Consultation.

The closing page: the atelier by the lake, the salon cities, and a booking form that asks for wrist size. The form is part of the concept, so it tells visitors that nothing was sent.

  • Parallax
  • Form

On Mobile

Every section holds at phone width. The pages run close to the edges, the type scales with each page, and both pinned scenes keep their timing. This is one continuous scroll from the first page to the booking form.

Imagery

All imagery was generated for this concept from a 15-shot prompt list, then converted to black and white with one shared contrast curve so it reads as a single shoot.
